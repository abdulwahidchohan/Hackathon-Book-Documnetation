## Overview

Humanoid robots require sophisticated hardware and sensor systems to perceive and interact with their environment. This chapter covers the essential components that enable physical embodiment and environmental awareness.

## Actuators and Motors

### Types of Actuators

**1. Electric Motors**
- **Brushless DC Motors**: High efficiency, precise control
- **Servo Motors**: Position feedback, common in joints
- **Stepper Motors**: Precise positioning without feedback

**2. Hydraulic Actuators**
- High force-to-weight ratio
- Used in Boston Dynamics Atlas
- Complex maintenance requirements

**3. Pneumatic Actuators**
- Compliant, safe for human interaction
- Lower precision than electric motors

### Joint Design

Humanoid robots typically have 20-44 degrees of freedom (DOF):
- **Head**: 2-3 DOF (pitch, yaw, roll)
- **Arms**: 7 DOF each (shoulder 3, elbow 1, wrist 3)
- **Hands**: 12-15 DOF (multi-fingered grasping)
- **Torso**: 2-3 DOF (waist rotation, bending)
- **Legs**: 6 DOF each (hip 3, knee 1, ankle 2)

## Vision Sensors

### RGB Cameras
- Standard color imaging
- Object recognition and tracking
- Typical resolution: 1080p-4K

### Depth Cameras
- **Stereo Cameras**: Triangulation-based depth
- **Time-of-Flight (ToF)**: Infrared light measurement
- **Structured Light**: Pattern projection (e.g., Intel RealSense)

### LiDAR
- 2D/3D environment mapping
- Range: 0.1m - 100m+
- Used for SLAM and obstacle avoidance

## Proprioceptive Sensors

### Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs)
- **Accelerometers**: Linear acceleration
- **Gyroscopes**: Angular velocity
- **Magnetometers**: Orientation reference
- Typical update rate: 100-1000 Hz

### Encoders
- **Absolute Encoders**: Position without homing
- **Incremental Encoders**: Relative position
- Resolution: 1000-10000 counts per revolution

### Force/Torque Sensors
- Measure contact forces
- Enable compliant control
- Critical for manipulation and walking

## Tactile Sensors

### Types
- **Resistive**: Pressure-sensitive materials
- **Capacitive**: Proximity and touch detection
- **Optical**: Camera-based tactile sensing (e.g., GelSight)

### Applications
- Grasp stability
- Object property detection
- Safe human-robot interaction

## Power Systems

### Battery Technologies
- **Lithium-Ion**: High energy density
- **Lithium-Polymer**: Flexible form factor
- Typical capacity: 2-5 kWh for humanoids
- Operating time: 2-4 hours

### Power Distribution
- Voltage regulation for different components
- Emergency shutdown systems
- Thermal management

## Computing Hardware

### Onboard Computers
- **CPU**: Intel i7/i9, AMD Ryzen
- **GPU**: NVIDIA Jetson (edge AI)
- **FPGA**: Real-time control loops

### Edge AI Accelerators
- NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin
- Google Coral TPU
- Intel Neural Compute Stick

## Communication Systems

### Wireless
- **WiFi**: High bandwidth, limited range
- **5G**: Low latency, wide coverage
- **Bluetooth**: Low power, short range

### Wired
- **Ethernet**: Reliable, high bandwidth
- **CAN Bus**: Motor controller communication
- **USB**: Peripheral connections

## Safety Systems

### Emergency Stop
- Hardware e-stop buttons
- Software-triggered stops
- Redundant safety circuits

### Collision Detection
- Torque monitoring
- Skin sensors
- Vision-based prediction

## Summary

Humanoid robot hardware integrates:
- **Actuators**: 20-44 DOF for human-like motion
- **Vision**: RGB, depth, LiDAR for perception
- **Proprioception**: IMUs, encoders, force sensors
- **Tactile**: Touch sensing for manipulation
- **Power**: 2-4 hour battery operation
- **Computing**: Edge AI for real-time processing
